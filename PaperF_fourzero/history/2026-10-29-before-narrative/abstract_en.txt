We study monic polynomials of even degree over F2 whose nonleading coefficients form a palindrome with constant term one and exactly four zeros. We determine their reciprocal common divisor and count the position pairs passing this obstruction. We classify their intersections with full-degree quadratic rational transformations: the translated Q branch comprises two infinite shape families and three degree-14 exceptions, while the translation-invariant branch comprises four reducible polynomials at degrees 14 and 30. More generally, a translation-invariant tail palindrome has n+2 a power of two and, for n >= 6, is reducible if its number of missing tail coefficients is divisible by four. The family-specific content is the compatibility of classical invariant representations and irreducibility criteria with the prescribed support, yielding restrictions on quadratic degree-doubling constructions. Independent arithmetic checks recover 2663 primitive polynomials at all even degrees from 6 to 200 and verify the first solutions through degree 300. General primitive existence remains unproved.
